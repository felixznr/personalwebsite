A few things I read this spring that I keep thinking about.

## The Passenger — Cormac McCarthy

McCarthy's late novels read like someone who spent a lifetime mastering prose and then decided to take it apart. *The Passenger* is slow, strange, and full of dialogues that feel like they were written for a play that will never be staged. I'm still not sure if I liked it. I'm sure it's important.

## Surely You're Joking, Mr. Feynman

I picked this up again after starting my thesis. Feynman's ability to see physics as play — as something fundamentally *fun* — is infectious. There's a passage about him watching a plate spin in the cafeteria that perfectly captures what it feels like when curiosity drives the work instead of obligation.

## Notes from Underground — Dostoevsky

Re-read this after a friend recommended it. The underground man is insufferable, contradictory, and uncomfortably relatable. Dostoevsky wrote the most precise portrait of overthinking two hundred years before anyone had a word for it.
