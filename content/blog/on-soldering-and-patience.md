There's a specific kind of frustration that comes from bridging two pins on a 48-pin QFP for the third time in a row. The flux is smoking, the tip is dirty, and you're questioning every life decision that led you to hand-soldering a part that was clearly designed for a pick-and-place machine.

And then, somewhere around attempt four, something shifts. You stop rushing. You clean the tip properly. You add flux. You drag the iron slowly, deliberately, and the solder flows exactly where it should. Every pin is clean. The bridges are gone.

Hardware has a way of forcing you into the present moment that software never does. You can't `Ctrl+Z` a lifted pad. There's no debugger for a cold joint. The feedback loop is immediate and physical — you either did it right or you didn't, and the multimeter will tell you which.

I think this is why I keep coming back to it. In a world that's increasingly abstract, there's something grounding about work where the quality of your attention directly determines the quality of the result.
