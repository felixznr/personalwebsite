I watched *Stalker* for the first time on a rainy Sunday in January, which feels like the only correct context for it. Tarkovsky doesn't make films you watch — he makes films you sit inside.

The premise is simple: three men enter a forbidden Zone where, supposedly, there's a room that grants your deepest wish. The journey takes nearly three hours. Almost nothing happens in the way you'd expect something to happen in a film. And yet you can't look away.

What struck me most is the ending. They reach the Room, and nobody goes in. Not because they can't, but because they're afraid of what they might actually want. The Writer says something to the effect that conscious wishes are one thing — but the Room grants what you want at the deepest level, and that might be something you can't live with knowing about yourself.

It's a film about the gap between who we think we are and who we actually are. The Zone isn't a place. It's the act of being honest with yourself, and the terror that comes with it.

I'll probably watch it again next winter.
